# Title and Claim Reassessment

Date: 2026-09-21 (Asia/Shanghai)

## Working title A

**Global genetic correlation masks mixed-direction local genetic sharing across ocular and systemic diseases**

Assessment: **preferred**. It describes the central result at the level supported by LDSC, SUPERGNOVA, and independent PLACO convergence. “Mixed-direction local genetic sharing” is more precise than a causal pleiotropy label, and “masks” refers to discordance between global and local summaries rather than to a biological mechanism.

## Working title B

**Opposing local genetic effects reveal hidden shared architecture between ocular and systemic diseases**

Assessment: acceptable with caution, but secondary. “Opposing local genetic effects” captures the negative-direction subset, while “reveal” is slightly stronger than the evidence because the locus-level functional annotations are not causal. If used, the abstract and title page should state that the effects are statistical directions under the frozen PLACO/SUPERGNOVA framework.

## Working title C

**Mixed-direction local genetic covariance shapes ocular-systemic comorbidity beyond global genetic correlation**

Assessment: not preferred. “Mixed-direction local genetic covariance” is supported, but “shapes comorbidity” can be read causally or mechanistically. It should not be used without independent shared-causal or longitudinal evidence.

## Restricted title

**Antagonistic pleiotropy shapes comorbidity among ocular and systemic diseases**

Assessment: not preferred at this stage. The project documents negative-direction local sharing and mixed-direction local genetic sharing, but the Phase 2B-R public-LD gate did not establish shared causal variants or genes. This title may be reconsidered only after independent causal evidence becomes available.

## Recommended wording

Use “negative-direction local sharing,” “positive-direction local sharing,” and “mixed-direction local genetic sharing.” Use “antagonistic pleiotropy” only as a hypothesis or interpretation requiring further causal validation.
